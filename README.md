# Project Overview

## Viewing

The `.stl` files can be opened up and viewed natively in Github. More images may be uploaded later, including images of the physical prints themselves

## Design Specifications

This headphone holder is designed to fit my pair of headphones, the Audio-Technica ATH-M20x (Closed-back). It is also designed to fit into my specific standing desk, but the CAD model could potentially be modified so that it can fit or be attached to something else, thereby still acting as a holder for the same headphones

## Explaining the CAD model

### Background

In the process of learning CAD, I felt that I needed a relevant project to test my engineering skills and to work through the process of designing something physically real. I wanted the experience of having to take real measurements, do some simple calculations, and most importantly iterate on a design to improve it over time. I eventually came up with the idea of designing a headphone holder for my desk, as my headphones were always sitting on top of my stack of papers, folders, and books that were on an adjacent table. 

### Functionality of the two parts

Part 1 is the main body which fits into two gaps in my desk snuggly. I took measurements and did multiple iterations to find the right tolerances so that the two protruding pieces fit snugly into the gaps in my desk. Part 2 is a piece that slides into a slot into the back to keep the first part firmly in place and to stop it from moving around as much as possible.

In V1, there is one open rectangular slot facing directly upward, and in V2 there are two of these recetngular slots at angles. The point of these slots is the same across both versions. The slot is used to hold a piece of foam which the top curved part of the headphones will rest on top of.
